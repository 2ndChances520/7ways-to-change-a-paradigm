7 Ways to Change a Paradigm

By James Sopok (Adapted for Web Study)

What is a Paradigm?

A Paradigm is a set of assumptions, concepts, values, and practices that constitutes a way of viewing reality for the community that shares them, especially in an intellectual discipline.

I've heard it described as: "It's a group of habits that are programmed in your subconscious mind that control your behavior." Sometimes called a "set point."

We have a number of paradigms in our lives: work, health, relationships, wealth, etc.

What Holds a Paradigm in Place?

  • Sense of Identity: Example: "I am" statements. If you're trying to lose weight and your self-identity is, "I'm a fat person, a greedy person," it's going to make it harder to lose weight. Sense of identity drives your beliefs and behaviors.
  • Beliefs: Thoughts like, "I'm not good enough," "I'm not worthy enough," can hold you back from achieving your goal. Beliefs about God can also be a powerful paradigm.
  • Consistent Thoughts: Your consistent thoughts, positive or negative, on that paradigm will ultimately influence your actions and that develops into habits.
  • Mental View of the World: Your image of the world. "Everyone's out to get me," "I can't trust anyone," versus "People are mostly good," "If I put in the right effort and I do the right things, anyone can be successful and the world will support me."
  • Self-Image: This is the lens you see yourself and the world through.

It's impossible to live your life without paradigms. You need a paradigm to learn to drive a car and master it so you can carry on a conversation and drive at the same time. Master a sport, master a piano. Even staying in bed, hitting the snooze button, and dreading what your day entails is a paradigm.

How are Paradigms Created?

It's important to understand how a paradigm is created to learn how to change or create new paradigms.

  • They come from habits growing up. These habits serve us or don't serve us and can come from what people told you: parents, teachers, and even the lyrics from music can influence our habits. This is very important before the age of 8! The part of the mind that criticizes and evaluates information may not be fully formed, so we tend to just accept things.
  • Life Experiences: Both good and bad ones, and more importantly, the meaning you give to them can have an influence on a paradigm.
  • Friends and Social Circle: The group of people you grew up with can have an influence.
  • Education: Your level of education or lack of education can influence it.
  • Repetition: Thinking the same thing, doing the same thing.
  • Core Beliefs You Have Around Life: Sense of enoughness, sense of self-worth, sense of safety, sense of control, and connecting with others.

The Conscious and Subconscious Mind

To understand how a paradigm is created, you need to learn more about the conscious mind and the subconscious mind.

The conscious mind is the ability to think in the moment, make decisions, solve problems, and also to act in the moment.

The subconscious mind has no ability to think and accepts most information in. It stores and records consistent decisions, actions, and habits. It's your subconscious mind that gives us the muscle memory to drive a car or play a sport without thinking about every single movement. Your subconscious mind is what may create your habits!

To change a paradigm, or to make a paradigm shift, involves changing your sense of who you are—your sense of identity in THAT area of life—then working on changing beliefs around that area, then changing your actions. Doing different actions, making different decisions, and to do that you often need to change the way you FEEL about that area/paradigm.

7 Ways to Change a Paradigm / Paradigm Shift

1. Identify Negative Paradigm

Really define it and then work out what the positive paradigm is. What kind of paradigm shift do you want in your life? Money, health, relationship, change yourself. One option is finding solutions internally, look within.

Examples:

  • Living in the present instead of the "dirty past."
  • Changing from lazy to active.
  • Shifting your thinking from "what I don't want" to "what I do want."
2. New Identity Statement

Craft a new identity statement. For example, drawing from scripture: "I'm a new creation! Behold, all things are new!" (2 Corinthians 5:17).

Other examples:

  • "I'm a motivated person that gets things done."
  • "I'm a health-minded person that will get fit."

Be aware of resistance to your new statement. You might need to modify your statement until you decrease the resistance you feel.

3. New Beliefs

Look at your new identity statement and come up with supporting beliefs. Sometimes it helps to identify negative beliefs to find solutions and create new positive ones. Consider your beliefs about Christ, the Trinity (3-in-1), eternal life, etc., as these are foundational.

4. Creative Visualization

Your subconscious mind cannot tell the difference between a real experience that's happened in your life and one that you vividly imagine. This can even affect your nervous system. Use all your senses in your visualization.

5. Repeat Affirmations

Turn negative affirmations into positive ones. The negative ones have been working too well; it's time to turn it around! Use "I am good at..." statements.

If you encounter large resistance, try using:

  • "I want to..." statements, then progress to
  • "I can..." statements.

Bob Proctor's example: "I am so happy and grateful now, that [money/blessings from Jesus] comes to me, in increasing quantities, through multiple sources, on a continuous basis." He recommends saying that 1000 times a day for 90 days and guarantees a profound change due to reprogramming your mind. (Consider the source and adapt prayerfully).

Thoughts? This is a great point for discussion in a Bible study: How do affirmations align with or differ from prayer and declaring God's promises?

6. Develop Faith

Biblical definition of faith: "Now faith is confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see." (Hebrews 11:1 NIV).

  • Trust in a process.
  • Trusting in a Higher Power (God).
  • Trusting a mentor.

Our analytical part of our brain, the egotistical part, will often have a problem with this. Focus on the change/want and not on the "how" or the process. Surrender the "how" to God.

7. Get Out of Your Comfort Zone!

Your comfort zone is comfortable for a reason; it's to keep fear away or the fear of the unknown, fear of rejection. Homeostasis (the tendency to maintain a stable internal state) can keep us stuck.

The solution is to take action anyway! Look at people who have achieved what you want to achieve. Look at people that have the paradigm you want to achieve and notice what those people are DOING. Start doing what they're doing even if it feels uncomfortable. That might help you change the paradigm in your life.
